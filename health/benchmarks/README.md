# Reproduction benchmarks (open tier)

Generated by `benchmarks/run.py` from `cases.toml`; do not edit by hand. Run `python benchmarks/run.py` from `health/` to refresh it.

Each case runs this tool with the curve, counterfactual and year of a published estimate, on the exposure map closest to the paper's, and compares the result with the published figure. Where they differ, the reason is written down; nothing is tuned to close a gap. Ratio is ours divided by published. Each case states the ratio it expects, worked out from the known differences in inputs and not from the result, and passes when the ratio is within a narrow band of that expectation: a case expected at 0.67 fails at 1.0.

## Why they differ

One paragraph per case. The full notes, with quotations from each source, are in `cases.toml` (`why_different`).

### jaganathan2024_share: Jaganathan et al. 2024

**Published:** 24.9% (95% CI 19.5-32.5) of total mortality attributable to PM2.5 above 5 µg/m³, 2009-19. Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11790315/>, Summary (Findings); Table 2, 'PM2.5 concentrations higher than WHO guidelines (≤5 μg/m3)', attributable fraction, both the 11-year total and 'Mean deaths per year' rows.

**Ours:** 26.0% of deaths, from `loglinear_jaganathan` on the `aqli` map, 2014, India (population-weighted 42.8 µg/m³), counterfactual 5 µg/m³.

Same curve and same formula as the paper. Our exposure is the AQLI map for 2014, the middle of the paper's 2009-19, which strips out dust and sea salt; the paper built its own monitor-calibrated map of total PM2.5 for every year. The shares land within 4% of each other.

**Band ±10%:** Same curve and formula; the exposure map and its year differ. Over 2011-19 our share runs from 25.2% to 28.4% on the AQLI map, 1% to 14% above the paper's 24.9%; 2014 is the middle year of the paper's period.

### jaganathan2024_deaths: Jaganathan et al. 2024

**Published:** 1.5 million (95% CI 1.1-1.9) deaths a year attributable to PM2.5 above 5 µg/m³, mean over 2009-19. Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11790315/>, Table 2, 'PM2.5 concentrations higher than WHO guidelines (≤5 μg/m3)', 'Mean deaths per year' row: 1·5 (1·1–1·9) million; Discussion: 'We estimated that 1·5 million (95% CI 1·1–1·9) deaths occurred annually'.

**Ours:** 2.19 M deaths/yr, from `loglinear_jaganathan` on the `aqli` map, 2014, India (population-weighted 42.8 µg/m³), counterfactual 5 µg/m³.

The paper counts only deaths registered with the Civil Registration System, about 6.1 million a year, and many deaths in India are never registered (completeness ran from 30% to 100% by state). We count all deaths: 8.4 million for 2014, at the SRS's own rate for that year. The shares nearly match, so the counts differ by about that ratio, 1.39 times, plus the 4% gap in the share. Compare the share, not the count.

**Expected ratio 1.392:** The paper's count is a share of the deaths registered in the Civil Registration System; ours is a share of all deaths. Its 16.6 million attributable deaths over 11 years at 24.9% imply 6.06 million registered deaths a year. Ours for 2014 is 8.44 million, at the SRS's own crude death rate for 2014 (6.7 per 1,000; data/death_reconciliation_by_year.csv). 8.44 / 6.06 = 1.392.

**Band ±8%:** The band holds the gap between the two shares (previous case, within 10%); the denominators are known.

### aqli2025_india_years: AQLI India fact sheet 2025

**Published:** India, 41 µg/m³ in 2023: meeting the WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³ 'could add 3.5 years onto the life expectancy of an average Indian'. Source: <https://aqli.epic.uchicago.edu/files/India%20FactSheet_2025_GlobalWV.pdf>, Page 1, opening paragraph and first key take-away; data year 2023 (footnote 1: 'AQLI 2023 dataset', population-weighted).

**Ours:** 3.55 years, from `aqli` on the `aqli` map, 2023, India (population-weighted 41.2 µg/m³), counterfactual 5 µg/m³.

Same exposure table, same coefficient (0.98 years per 10 µg/m³) and same 5 µg/m³ target as AQLI. Our districts are weighted by the 2023 population projection of their state (AQLI's split within each state), which puts India at 41.2 µg/m³ against AQLI's 41.0; that and AQLI's rounding to one decimal are all that separate the two.

**Band ±3%:** Same table, coefficient and target; AQLI prints one decimal (3.5 is 3.45 to 3.55, ±1.4%) and weights districts by its own population.

### aqli2025_delhi_years: AQLI India fact sheet 2025

**Published:** NCT of Delhi, 88.4 µg/m³ in 2023: 8.2 years of life expectancy gained from meeting the WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³. Source: <https://aqli.epic.uchicago.edu/files/India%20FactSheet_2025_GlobalWV.pdf>, Page 3, table 'Potential life expectancy impacts of particulate pollution reductions in all states/UTs of India', row 'NCT of Delhi' (population 188.4 lakh, 88.4 µg/m³, 8.2 years); also page 1, last key take-away ('gaining 8.2 years of life expectancy').

**Ours:** 8.17 years, from `aqli` on the `aqli` map, 2023, Delhi (population-weighted 88.3 µg/m³), counterfactual 5 µg/m³.

As for India: same table, coefficient and target, so only rounding separates them. Delhi here is the state (NCT of Delhi), as in AQLI's table.

**Band ±3%:** As for India; 8.2 is 8.15 to 8.25, ±0.6%.

### burnett2018_india_maps: Burnett et al. 2018 (GEMM NCD+LRI), India 2015, on our map

**Published:** India 2015, population-weighted PM2.5 74.0 µg/m³, 100% rollback: 2,219 thousand excess deaths (GEMM NCD+LRI). Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6156628/>, Table 1, row 'India', rollback 100%: PM2.5 exposure 74.0 µg/m3, GEMM NCD+LRI 2,219 (thousands). The authors' deposited calculator gives 2,219,057 (see data/sources.toml, curves_gemm_ncd_lri.csv).

**Ours:** 1.31 M deaths/yr, from `gemm_ncd_lri` on the `aqli` map, 2015, India (population-weighted 45.1 µg/m³), counterfactual 2.4 µg/m³.

Same curve, year and counterfactual, but our map puts India's 2015 air at 45.1 µg/m³ against the paper's 74.0 (AQLI removes dust and sea salt and uses a different model), which alone cuts the deaths to about three-quarters. Our baseline also has a quarter fewer deaths from the causes the curve covers than the paper's (next case). Together: a little over half.

**Expected ratio 0.619:** Two known differences multiply. Baseline deaths: the paper's are GBD 2015's, 6.46 million deaths from non-communicable disease and lower respiratory infection at ages 25 and over; ours for 2015 are 5.27 million, 0.816 of that (next case). Exposure: our map puts India at 45.1 µg/m³ against the paper's 74.0; on the paper's own baseline the curve gives 1.68 million deaths at 45.1 against 2.22 million at 74.0, a factor of 0.758. 0.816 × 0.758 = 0.619.

**Band ±8%:** The band holds what the two factors leave out: exposure varies within India and the curve bends, so applying it district by district gives about 4% fewer deaths than applying it once at the national mean.

### burnett2018_india_forced74: Burnett et al. 2018 (GEMM NCD+LRI), India 2015, at the paper's 74.0 µg/m³

**Published:** India 2015, population-weighted PM2.5 74.0 µg/m³, 100% rollback: 2,219 thousand excess deaths (GEMM NCD+LRI). Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6156628/>, Table 1, row 'India', rollback 100%: PM2.5 exposure 74.0 µg/m3, GEMM NCD+LRI 2,219 (thousands).

**Ours:** 1.82 M deaths/yr, from `gemm_ncd_lri` on the `aqli` map, 2015, India (every district forced to 74 µg/m³), counterfactual 2.4 µg/m³.

With every district set to the paper's 74.0 µg/m³ we land 18% below, and the reason is known: the paper's baseline (GBD 2015) has 6.46 million deaths at ages 25 and over from the causes the curve covers, ours has 5.27 million. Given the paper's own baseline, this tool's curve reproduces the paper's 2,219,057 deaths exactly. GBD counts more deaths in India than the SRS does, most of all at 80 and over, and our counts are scaled to the SRS's own crude death rate for 2015 (6.5 per 1,000).

**Expected ratio 0.816:** At the paper's exposure the only difference left is baseline deaths. The paper's are in the authors' workbook (sheet 'NCD+ LRI baseline': GBD 2015, from the GHDx results tool, retrieved 31 July 2017): India, ages 25 and over, 6,457,516 deaths from non-communicable disease and lower respiratory infection. Ours for 2015: 5,269,644. 5.270 / 6.458 = 0.816. Two things make ours smaller. Before any scaling it is 5,860,966 (0.908): GBD counts more deaths at 80 and over than the SRS, and our cause shares are too low at 25-29. Scaling deaths at 45 and over to the SRS crude death rate for 2015 takes it down by a further 10%.

**Band ±5%:** The band holds the difference in the age pattern of the two baselines (the curve is steeper at younger ages); the totals are known.
