# Reading list

Ordered by what to read first, not by importance. ★ = start here.

## 1. The argument about markets

**★ [Anant Sudarshan on Market Solutions to Air Pollution, Energy Policy, and Ecological
Disruption](https://www.mercatus.org/ideasofindia/anant-sudarshan-market-solutions-air-pollution-energy-policy-and-ecological-disruption)** —
Ideas of India ep. 126, Shruti Rajagopalan, 16 Jan 2025. The single best entry point.
Sudarshan is the person who turned "measure it properly" into policy in India: he helped
form the committee that wrote India's first CEMS technical standards in 2013, then ran the
Surat market. Rajagopalan's framing in the episode is sharp — the largest share of north
Indian pollution comes from agriculture, which is also the least productive part of the
economy.

**★ [Altruism and Development — It's complicated](https://srajagopalan.substack.com/p/altruism-and-development-its-complicated)** —
Shruti Rajagopalan. The piece from the thread that started this.

**[Research page](https://www.anantsudarshan.com/papers/)** — Sudarshan. The whole programme
in one place.

**[Monitoring Industrial Pollution in India](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/174156/1/WRAP-monitoring-industrial-pollution-India-Sudarshan-2022.pdf)** —
Sudarshan, 2022 book chapter. Why Indian environmental regulation fails in practice:
not the rules, the counting.

## 2. The Surat experiment

The world's first particulate emissions market, and the only intervention in this whole
space with a randomised trial behind it.

- **The QJE paper** (Greenstone, Pande, Ryan, Sudarshan). ~320 plants with continuous
  monitors, randomised into a cap-and-trade market vs status quo regulation, 2019–21.
  **20–30% emissions cut, ~11% lower compliance costs, profits up, and mortality benefits
  exceeding costs by at least 25×.**
- [J-PAL scaling page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/aspire-our-work-scaling-impact-emissions-trading-scheme) —
  what happened after: Gujarat extended it to Ahmedabad in 2022; Earthshot finalist 2025.
- [EPIC summary](https://epic.uchicago.edu/insights/in-the-midst-of-an-air-pollution-crisis-theres-another-way-india-can-better-enforce-emission-rules/)
- [Yale EGC on emissions markets](https://egc.yale.edu/news/250723/emissions-markets-reduce-air-pollution)

## 3. The philanthropic view

- **[South Asian Air Quality cause investigation](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/WxpAGzLvBLrZgmTFs/south-asian-air-quality-cause-investigation)** —
  Open Philanthropy's pre-launch report. Judged the space able to absorb **$25M/yr at
  competitive cost-effectiveness against ~$7M/yr of total philanthropic spending.** Read it
  for the reasoning, and note the worry about crowding out other funders.
- Programme launched Jan 2022 under Santosh Harish; scope widened in Oct 2023 to lead,
  alcohol and suicide. Open Philanthropy was **renamed Coefficient Giving in Nov 2025**, so
  grant pages now appear under the new name.
- Worth noticing: the grants are almost entirely **governance and state capacity** —
  staffing pollution control boards, truck emissions studies. Not one physical moonshot.

## 4. The physics — why the air sits still

- **[Modeling of the Effects of Wintertime Aerosols on Boundary Layer Properties Over the
  Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018jd029758)** —
  Bharali et al., JGR 2019. Aerosol forcing at the surface of **40–80 W/m²** measurably
  suppresses the boundary layer. The core of the dome effect over the IGP.
- **[Role of atmospheric aerosols in severe winter fog over the Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/6635/2024/)** —
  ACP 2024. Observational, Dec 2017 fog event: feedback weakens turbulence, lowers PBL
  height, raises PM2.5 and humidity together.
- **[Meteorological modulation of ambient air quality in Delhi and Lahore, Nov 2024 haze](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-63281-w)** —
  Nature Sci Rep. PBL height vs PM2.5 at **r = −0.39** in Delhi.
- **[IGP experiences higher aerosol pollution than the North China Plain despite lower
  emissions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11356-026-38305-7)** — the comparison
  that proves meteorology and feedback, not just emissions, set the level. Probably the
  most important single paper for the "can we engineer the air?" question.

## 5. What's actually polluting

- **[What Is Polluting Delhi's Air? A Review from 1990 to 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/5/4209)** —
  Guttikunda et al. The standard reference; read before trusting any single apportionment.
- **[Beyond the burn: Delhi's winter smog intensifies even after stubble fires fade](https://www.cseindia.org/beyond-the-burn-delhi-s-winter-smog-intensifies-even-after-stubble-fires-fade-12982)** —
  CSE. Local sources only ~35% of Delhi's PM2.5 in Dec 2025; the airshed is the unit, not the city.
- **[Is Ex-situ Crop Residue Management a Scalable Solution to Stubble Burning?](https://www.ceew.in/publications/ex-situ-crop-residue-management-scalable-solution-for-paddy-stubble-burning)** —
  CEEW. The supply-chain economics: a 15–20 day collection window, 15–25% of stored bales
  lost, and only 3% of farmers paid anything.
- **[Punjab and Haryana: Diversification to Augment Farmers' Incomes](https://icrier.org/pdf/ES_diversification-to-augment-farmers-incomes-and-promote-sustainable-agriculture-in-punjab-and-haryana.pdf)** —
  ICRIER. Costs out paying Punjab to stop growing rice: ~₹35,000/ha for 5 years, largely
  self-funding from fertiliser and power subsidy savings.

## 6. The health burden

- **[Black carbon health impacts in the Indo-Gangetic plain](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abo4093)** —
  Science Advances. **BC-focused planning ≈ 400,000 lives saved vs ≈ 80,000 for PM2.5-mass
  planning.** India has no BC target at all. The biggest unclaimed policy lever on the board.
- **[Black Carbon-Attributable Mortality in India](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13179582/)** —
  133 all-cause deaths per 100,000/yr; ~1.2M premature deaths avertable.
- **[AQLI methodology](https://aqli.epic.uchicago.edu/about/methodology/)** — Greenstone's
  life-expectancy index, built on the Huai River natural experiment. Roughly **0.98 years
  of life lost per 10 µg/m³**. The number everyone quotes; worth understanding its basis.

## 7. Interventions, evaluated

- **[Air Pollution in Delhi: Does GRAP Grip at All?](http://www.isid.ac.in/~acegd/acegd2024/papers/PaulKattuman.pdf)** —
  Kattuman & Harvey, 2024. **No significant effect for GRAP stages 1, 3 and 4**; a positive
  effect only for stage 2. Their explanation: GRAP is reactive, triggering after peaks.
  Conflicts with an IITM/CAQM counterfactual claiming 60% more severe hours without it.
- **[Judicial prohibition on firecrackers and Delhi air quality](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11356-021-17695-w)** —
  PM2.5 down ~50% in 2017, up ~32% in 2018. Enforcement, not the rule.
- **[Firework bans across the IGP airshed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1352231022002886)** —
  the 2020 ban looks more effective; regional rather than city-level.
- **[Cloud seeding is not a proven solution for air pollution](https://india.mongabay.com/2025/11/cloud-seeding-not-a-proven-solution-for-air-pollution/)** —
  Mongabay, Nov 2025. The autopsy on Delhi's ₹3.21 crore trial. Ghude's estimate: going from
  "very poor" to "moderate" would need rain almost every other day.
- **[Smog towers ineffective; 40,000 needed](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/pollution/smog-towers-ineffective-at-least-40-000-needed-in-delhi-to-combat-toxic-air-dpcc-tells-ngt-92841)** —
  DPCC to the NGT.

## 8. The data and its limits

Read this before trusting any Indian AQI number, including ours.

- **[CAG performance audit of Delhi air quality monitoring](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2021/6-Chapter-2-067ebb3e72ff8b4.30490563.pdf)** —
  **all 13 Delhi CAAQMS had trees on multiple sides**; Anand Vihar and Wazirpur sit beside
  heavy-vehicle roads. CPCB's own rule is >20 m from trees. The most authoritative siting
  critique that exists.
- **[Status of air quality monitoring in India](https://www.cseindia.org/Note-AQM-Network-analysis.pdf)** —
  CSE. ~860 million people live outside a 50 km radius of any real-time station; only 12% of
  census towns have one.
- **[Comprehensive Monitoring of Air Pollution Hotspots Using Sparse Sensor Networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04309)** —
  the low-cost-sensor answer to the coverage problem. Sudarshan is on this line of work too.
