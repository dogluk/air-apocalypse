Bengaluru: from the air to what could work

This page follows the air of one city, Bengaluru, from the ground to what could be done about it. It is written to understand, not to persuade. Where published sources disagree, the page shows them side by side and does not average them or pick a winner.

It has four chapters. 1. The air itself: what the monitors on the ground and the satellite maps say about PM2.5, the fine particles (smaller than 2.5 micrometres across) that do most of the harm to health, measured in micrograms per cubic metre of air (µg/m³). 2. Where it comes from: what the studies of the city's air say the particles came from. 3. What it does to health: the deaths and years of life that PM2.5 takes, on every published way of turning air into health. 4. What could work, and what it would buy: measures the city could take, with the odds each works and what it would cost.

The air itself

This chapter is about what the air holds, measured two ways. The government's ground monitors (the Central Pollution Control Board, CPCB, and the state boards that run them) read the air at a fixed spot every hour. Satellite maps estimate it everywhere from space. The main pollutant here is PM2.5: particles under 2.5 micrometres across, small enough to reach deep into the lungs. It is counted in µg/m³, micrograms of particles in a cubic metre of air. An annual mean is the average over a whole year.

Satellites against monitors

Each series is its own source, never pooled:

  • CPCB ground monitors, city annual means
  • AQLI 2025 release, districts, satellite PM2.5 with dust and sea salt removed
  • ACAG V6.GL.02.04 total PM2.5, districts, WorldPop-weighted

AQLI removes mineral dust and sea salt from the satellite estimate; ACAG keeps them; CPCB is the monitors' median across the city's stations. The GBD (Global Burden of Disease) maps are state-level only, so they have no city value and are not drawn here.

In 2023, the latest year all of them cover, the values were CPCB monitors 29.1, AQLI 26.2, ACAG 31.9 µg/m³. They are listed, not averaged: the gap between them is itself a finding.

Where it comes from

A source study asks what the particles in a city's air came from. There are two kinds. An emissions model starts from what is burned (a list of vehicles, chimneys, fires) and computes where the smoke goes. A receptor model starts from what landed on a filter and works backwards to the likely sources. They answer slightly different questions and they disagree. They are shown side by side here, not averaged.

What it does to health

This chapter turns the air into health. A risk curve is a published estimate of how much the chance of dying rises as the yearly PM2.5 level rises. An exposure map says what level people in a place breathe. Put the two together and you get the deaths attributable to the air: the deaths a year that would not happen if the air were clean down to the curve's floor, the level below which the curve counts no further harm. Life-years add up the years of life those deaths took, each death counted by the years the person had left. Life expectancy gained is how much longer the average person would live.

The curves do not agree, and neither do the maps. The PM2.5 level for Bengaluru is cpcb 27.9 µg/m³ (2025); aqli 26.2 µg/m³ (2023); acag_total 31.9 µg/m³ (2023). Across every curve and map below, the deaths a year attributable to the air run from 1,415 to 12,412. The gap comes from the curves (each was fitted to different studies, some mostly of cleaner air than India's) and from the maps (they measure the air differently). No curve or map is picked here, and nothing is averaged.

What could work, and what it would buy

A measure is one thing the city could do about its air, written as a file in the measure library with every number's source. There are two kinds. An air measure cuts a cause of PM2.5 (dust, vehicles, burning) and so cleans the outdoor air for everyone; how much it cleans depends on how big that cause is, which comes from a source study (chapter 2). An exposure measure (purifiers, masks) leaves the outdoor air as it is and cuts what some people breathe, for some of their hours.

Each measure has odds that it works: the chance it is carried out and does what it says, mostly a judgement written down with its reasons. Figures marked if it works assume it does; expected figures multiply the life-years by those odds (and the cost too, for the part paid only on success). Every figure is a Monte Carlo range: the measure is run many times with its uncertain inputs (how big the cut is, the curve's own parameters, the cost) drawn afresh each time; the median is the middle run and the range covers the middle 90% of runs. Life-years per crore is life-years gained for every crore (ten million rupees) spent: higher means more health for the money.

Every measure is scored on one exposure map, acag_total (ACAG V6.GL.02.04 total PM2.5, districts, WorldPop-weighted), where Bengaluru's PM2.5, weighted by where people live, is 31.9 µg/m³ in 2023. Chapter 1's ground monitors read the city's air differently, and chapter 3 shows the maps side by side; this chapter uses the map the ranking across cities uses.

Air measures are scored on every source study of Bengaluru that splits PM2.5 by cause (apna_2015, cstep_bengaluru2022_cmb), each on its own; the studies disagree, and their results are never combined. Exposure measures do not depend on a study and are scored once.

Curves used: loglinear_jaganathan (Log-linear, Jaganathan 2024 (Indian districts, all-cause, all ages)); loglinear_brown (Log-linear, Brown 2022 Million Death Study (non-accidental; studied at ages 15-69, applied from 15, extrapolated above 69)); loglinear_vodonos (Log-linear, Vodonos 2018 meta-regression (all-age all-cause in the paper, applied from age 25: the pooled studies are adult cohorts), slope at a study mean of 30 µg/m³ (the published slope falls with concentration; one slope misstates it)); gemm_ncd_lri (GEMM NCD+LRI (Burnett 2018), age-specific); aqli (AQLI linear, 0.98 years of life expectancy per 10 µg/m³); gbd2019 (GBD 2019 MR-BRT relative risks, six causes, IHD and stroke by age (IHME, local)); gbd2023 (GBD 2023 relative risks, seven causes, no age split (IHME, local)); gemm_5cod (GEMM five causes (Burnett 2018), IHD and stroke by age, with IHME deaths (local)). 5,000 runs per curve, seed 0, life-years and costs discounted at 3% a year.

What the scores assume

  • Share times cut: cutting a cause of PM2.5 by a given percent is taken to cut its share of the city's PM2.5 by the same percent.
  • No chemistry: real air is not additive (particles formed in the air from gases, limits set by ammonia, the dome over a city), and none of that is modelled.
  • Health only: benefits to the climate, crops or time saved are not counted, and nothing is valued in money.
  • Life-years and costs are counted over the measure's years plus the years in which its effect on deaths keeps arriving (20 under the default lag), discounted at 3% a year; deaths postponed are not discounted.
  • Cost is what a government or funder would spend (the funder's rupees), in 2025 rupees; a measure that saves society money overall is listed apart, not ranked.

Who it reaches: everyone in India downwind of a coal power plant, through the outdoor air.

Odds that it works: 60%. Basis: judgement: scrubbers are proven and some already run in India, and paying for them removes the plants' main objection, but the 2015 sulphur limits have been postponed again and again, and a fitted scrubber still has to be run, which costs the plant power.

PM2.5 change if it works, in the city average (the yearly mean, weighted by where people live), from the part of the measure that reaches the city: -1.60 µg/m³ (-2.20 to -1.01).

This measure acts on a source spread over several states, and most of its benefit falls outside the city. The figures here are only the part of its life-years that reaches the city. Its cost and its life-years per crore belong to its whole reach (every district it changes), so they are not given on this page; the ranking gives them (airhealth score --region fgd_scrubbers).

Cost: belongs to the whole region the measure reaches, not to this city, so it is not given here.
