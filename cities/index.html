Eleven cities: the air, what it does, what could work

One row for each of the eleven cities the Diwali track follows. Each city has its own page that goes from the air to health to what could be done about it; this table gives the headline numbers from those pages side by side. It is not a ranking: the rows follow the order of the repository's city list, nothing is averaged across cities, and no column is a score.

How to read the columns

  • PM2.5 on CPCB monitors: the yearly mean of PM2.5, the fine particles (under 2.5 micrometres across) that do most of the harm, in micrograms per cubic metre of air (µg/m³), as read by the Central Pollution Control Board's ground monitors in the latest year the city has enough data for (at least 75% of that year's hours). The year is in brackets. Years of monitor data counts the years the city has such a reading; the eleven cities have up to 10, and Mumbai (9), Kolkata (7), Ahmedabad (8), Pune (8), Surat (2), Jaipur (8) fewer, so their latest year rests on a shorter record.
  • PM2.5 on the ACAG satellite map: the same yearly mean estimated from space, weighted by where people live, for the satellite map's own latest year. It is a different instrument and often a different year; the two columns are side by side, not averaged.
  • Range of deaths a year: the deaths a year that PM2.5 above each risk curve's floor accounts for, from the lowest to the highest answer over 8 risk curves and 3 exposure maps. The published curves and maps disagree several-fold and no one of them is preferred, so a range is all that is given. The city pages say which curve and map sit at each end.
  • Source studies: the studies in health/data/causes.csv that say where the city's PM2.5 comes from, counted by study id. A study with several parts (a model run for different years, a receptor study next to a model) counts once for each part, so this is a count of rows of evidence, not of research teams.
  • Library measures that apply: how many of the 22 measures in the measure library apply to the city. The library is written from single-city evidence, so a measure applies only where its file names the city (or says it fits every city), or where it acts on a whole region. Each city page lists which apply and why the rest do not.
