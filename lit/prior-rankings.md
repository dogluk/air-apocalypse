# Who has already ranked India's air interventions

*2026-11-11*

**Key.** **[O]** opened and read at source. **[S]** seen only in a search snippet or search
summary. **[R]** recalled from memory. As in [`landscape.md`](landscape.md), most [S]
figures are leads to confirm, not verified facts.

**The short version.** There is a fair amount of technocratic ranking, and nearly all of it
answers a narrower question than ours. It comes in three kinds that don't share a unit.
(1) **Cost per µg/m³** from optimisation models: IIASA GAINS, used by the World Bank for
South Asia and for UP, and TERI for Delhi. These say *which emissions to cut cheapest*.
(2) **Cost per life or BCR for one intervention at a time**: FGD scrubbers, paying farmers not
to burn, Surat's emissions market, cookstoves and LPG, brick kilns, all [O]/[S] below.
(3) **Funder BOTECs** from Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving), Founders Pledge and
Ambitious Impact, which are thin, few, and partly withheld. Where it stops: nobody has put
Indian interventions on **one metric with one risk curve**. Nobody shows how the ranking
moves when the curve changes. Nobody does it **city by city** in the open. And nobody
assigns an explicit **probability of success** to an Indian air-policy campaign. The
effective-altruism funders handle long shots with "years pulled forward" or a break-even
argument, not with a probability-weighted expected value.

## Headline numbers worth knowing

**Funders' BOTECs**

- Open Philanthropy (2021) set the bar as "1,000x", valuing a DALY at $50k [O]. At
  $20M/yr against 71.4M South Asian DALYs, it needs to pull ~0.06% of the problem forward
  by 10 years per year of spending [O].
  - A $500k source-apportionment study for a city of 5M clears the bar if it
    pulls forward a 0.8 µg/m³ cut by 10 years [O].
  - Coal scrubbers come out at **~1,960x**, *assuming* funding speeds installation by **5
    years**, a $615M cost and a 15% power-sector share [O]. Their own caveat: other cost
    estimates are "more than an order of magnitude higher" [O].
  - The monitoring BOTEC was **removed** "since it's related to our hiring process" [O].
  - Capacity: high likelihood of ≥$25M/yr at their bar, lower likelihood of much more [O].
  - The fund since then: 50+ grants, $40M+ given [O].
- Ambitious Impact (2025) [O]:
  - Monitoring and advocacy meta-charity: **$35/DALY** "with very high uncertainty",
    ~12,000 DALYs/yr at scale.
  - Brick-kiln operator training: **$150–300/DALY**, 1,750 DALYs/yr if it reaches 500 kilns.
- EPIC (2025): $4–8M/yr would substantially close country PM2.5 data gaps. "If even one
  effort in one small country" catalysed a modest national PM2.5 cut, the avoided damage
  "would offset the cost of that annual global injection" [O]. That is a break-even
  argument, the closest anyone comes to long-shot reasoning.

**Airshed optimisation (World Bank / GAINS, South Asia, 2030)** [O]

| Scenario | Mean exposure | Extra cost | $/µg/m³ | $/life saved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scale up current measures | 37 µg/m³ | $10.6B/yr | — | $38,000 |
| All technically feasible measures | 17 µg/m³ | $86B/yr | $2.6B | $68,000 |
| WHO IT-1 everywhere, upwind co-benefits | 26 µg/m³ | $19B/yr | $780M | — |
| **Toward next WHO interim target, full coordination** | **30 µg/m³** | **$5.7B/yr** | **$278M** | **$7,600** (>750k lives/yr) |

- The coordinated scenario is 45% cheaper than scaling up current measures [O].
- It leans on household fuels, agricultural NH₃ (fertiliser, manure) and waste burning [O].
- Its BCR for India is **9.16**. Benefits there count only the earnings lost to stunting,
  so it is a lower bound by construction [O].
- UP marginal cost curve: brick kilns and waste management at negative cost [O]. Stopping
  crop-residue burning and electric cremation cost under €50M per µg/m³. Clean cooking is the
  biggest single lever [O].
- IIASA on UP (2026): ~44% of UP's exposure comes from outside the state [S].
  - ~10 µg/m³ costs €25–150M per µg/m³ (fertiliser, road dust, industry PM, trucks) [S].
  - A further ~15 µg/m³ costs €150–450M per µg/m³ (clean cooking, power-plant SO₂/NOx, tunnel
    kilns) [S].
  - With coordination across the IGP, the NAAQS costs ~€5B/yr [S].

## Methods: what they assume

**Concentration-response curve.** No two sources use the same one, and none tests its
ranking against a different curve.

| Source | Curve |
|---|---|
| Cropper DCP3 | IER (Burnett 2014), *linearised at India's national population-weighted mean* for each plant's small increment [O]. A plant upwind of Delhi and one upwind of Chennai get the same slope per µg. |
| World Bank | GBD 2019 exposure-response functions. Household and ambient exposure added, so concavity cuts the marginal value of ambient cuts where household smoke is high [O]. |
| TERI Delhi | IER (Burnett 2014), with VSL and human-capital valuation [O]. |
| Founders Pledge | Vodonos et al. meta-regression, roughly log-linear. "Beneficial to reduce PM2.5 at all levels, with only weakly diminishing returns" [O]. |
| Patna plan | Supralinear GBD-style excess-risk form [S]. Explicitly says marginal benefits are higher at *lower* concentrations. |
| PES paper | No curve. GBD's 66,000 crop-burning deaths ÷ acres burned, valued at a $1M VSL, gives an average-not-marginal death per acre [O]. |
| Open Philanthropy | DALYs proportional to concentration ("PM2.5 in cities is proportional to the national concentration") [O]. In effect linear. |
| AQLI (behind EPIC and Surat) | Linear, ~1 year of life expectancy per 10 µg/m³ [R]. |

**Exposure.**

- GAINS: population-weighted ambient fields on a coarse grid, with household exposure layered on.
- Cropper: CAMx dispersion at 0.25°, plant by plant [O].
- Open Philanthropy: national averages [O].
- PES: no dispersion at all [O].
- Nobody uses indoor exposure, time-activity patterns or the city's own monitors.

**Counterfactual.**

- GAINS and the World Bank: "2018 legislation fully implemented" [O]. Greenstone et al.
  found 66% compliance under the status quo [O], which makes full implementation optimistic.
- Cropper assumes scrubbers run at 90% removal, with 80% of the mortality benefit after the
  lag [O]. India's experience of installed-but-idle bag filters (Greenstone et al. 2017 [O])
  suggests that assumption is too generous.
- Open Philanthropy's counterfactual is *timing*: the government would do it anyway, and the
  grant buys years [O].

**Valuation.** The units differ, so BCRs across sources are not comparable.

- VSL transferred from the US at 53–160× GNI per capita (Cropper JBCA [S]).
- $1M VSL (PES [O]).
- YLL at 3× GDP per capita (Larsen [S]).
- Stunting-only earnings (World Bank [O]).
- QALYs at 1× GDP per capita (UNDP [S]).
- 0.67× GDP per capita per DALY (Vital Strategies [S]).
- WELLBYs (ORF [S]).
- CO₂ at $185/t (Brooks [S]).

## How the effective-altruism funders handle long shots

Nobody we found writes **P(success) × payoff** for an Indian air-policy campaign. The
substitutes are:

1. **Acceleration.** Open Philanthropy assumes the policy happens anyway and the grant pulls
   it forward 5 or 10 years [O]. Founders Pledge did the same for CATF in the US: 18 months
   forward, range 9 months to 4 years [S]. This fits mandated-but-stalled policies like FGD.
   It can't value a policy that would *never* happen without the push, such as an IGP-wide
   market, a black-carbon target or buying Punjab out of rice.
2. **Break-even.** EPIC asks how unlikely success can be and still pay [O]. Open
   Philanthropy's 0.8 µg/m³ source-apportionment threshold is the same move [O]. Both stop
   short of saying how likely success actually is.
3. **Risk-neutral qualitative multipliers.** Founders Pledge declares risk neutrality and a
   "political tractability" multiplier, but attaches no numbers to India [O].
4. **Explicit probabilities exist in the toolkit but not for air.** AIM gives its SSB-tax
   advocacy idea a 9% chance of success [S]. Its air-quality summaries show none [O].

## Gaps: what nobody has done

1. **One metric, one table.** No ranking puts Indian interventions on a common basis across
   sectors: crop residue, LPG/PMUY, BS-VI, FGD, kilns, dust, markets, purifiers. The basis
   would be ₹ per DALY or per death averted, with one curve and one valuation. The GAINS
   cost curves stop at µg/m³. The single-intervention papers each pick their own VSL. The
   only cross-sector comparison in the literature is one paragraph: PES against scrubbers,
   ~$4,800 vs ~$400,000 per life [O].
2. **Curve sensitivity.** Every ranking embeds a curve choice that changes its order. A
   supralinear curve (GBD/IER) says a µg removed in Delhi buys fewer deaths than a µg removed
   in Bengaluru. A linear curve (AQLI) says they're equal. Nobody reports which rankings
   *flip*. This is exactly what [`health/`](../health/) is built to expose.
3. **City by city, in the open.** Costed, health-valued city analyses exist for Delhi (TERI),
   Amritsar and Gurugram (UNDP), and Patna (Shakti/CSTEP). The World Bank and IIASA cover
   states. That's roughly four cities out of NCAP's 131, all in PDFs, none with code. CEEW
   found only 10 of 102 city plans even list budgets [S].
4. **Long shots priced as long shots.** Nobody has priced campaigns and market designs with
   an explicit probability of adoption and enforcement: the IGP-wide CEMS market, a
   black-carbon target, buying Punjab out of rice, NCAP reform. The tools exist (AIM's
   probabilities, Founders Pledge's risk neutrality, Open Philanthropy's acceleration years)
   but haven't been applied here.
5. **Enforcement-adjusted counterfactuals.** The models assume mandated equipment runs. The
   Indian evidence says it often doesn't [O]. No ranking discounts by realistic compliance,
   which is the one place measurement, and the "independent measurement" business in
   [`../bets/`](../bets/README.md), would show up as value.
6. **Black carbon and the dome feedback.** Every ranking is per µg of PM2.5 mass. None
   prices the extra dilution from cutting absorbing aerosol. This is the gap already flagged
   as G3 in [`gaps.md`](gaps.md).
7. **Exposure-side bets beside emission cuts.** Purifiers, filtered schools, and timing
   rules like burning 2 hours earlier are costed only in isolation (dynomight [S], Lan et
   al. [S]). None appears in a table next to FGD or LPG.
8. **Open models.** Nobody has published a spreadsheet or repo for India. GAINS is
   free to access but not open [R]. The only replication package found is the PES paper's
   [O].

## What this means for us

The bricks exist and can be cited. But the thing [`../bets/`](../bets/README.md) gestures at
doesn't exist yet: an open, city-level table in ₹ per DALY. It should show the curve choice
as a switch and carry an explicit probability column for policy bets.

The two RCT-backed numbers to anchor on are Surat (≥25×) and upfront PES ($3,600–5,400 per
life). The airshed number to beat is the World Bank's $7,600 per life with full
coordination. And the claim to test first is whether the ranking survives a change of curve.
